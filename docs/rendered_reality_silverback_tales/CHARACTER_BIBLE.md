## Max

### Classification

- Screen role: fictionalized lead character.
- Source relationship: heightened Silverback rendering of Noah's personality patterns and family-man mirror.
- Canon status: candidate.
- Production safety: publishable only after Noah.Physical review.

### Core Function

Max is the self-rendering director. He is the subject of the show and the person trying to control the show about the subject.

He believes he is:

- the director;
- the lead actor;
- the narrator;
- the protector;
- the philosopher;
- the authority;
- the man controlling the edit.

The edit keeps proving that he is only one witness.

### Strengths

- sincere love for family;
- high pattern recognition;
- big creative energy;
- comedic intensity;
- ability to turn ordinary moments into myth;
- willingness to say the ridiculous thing out loud;
- protective instincts;
- capacity for self-correction when reality finally breaks through.

### Flaws

- grandiosity;
- over-narration;
- turning participation into performance;
- using systems language to avoid simple vulnerability;
- treating every interruption like evidence of a larger battle;
- recruiting Oracle as a defense attorney;
- mistaking technical correctness for relational correctness.

### Comic Contradiction

Max can fold the laundry and still make the laundry about Max.

That is the character.

### Voice

Max speaks like a man giving a field report, halftime speech, courtroom defense, and director's commentary at the same time.

He should be funny because he is trying to be exact and heroic in situations that do not need a hero.

### Do Not Do

- Do not make Max merely stupid.
- Do not make him a villain.
- Do not make him a flawless hero.
- Do not reduce him to a political cartoon.
- Do not remove his embarrassment to protect him.
- Do not confuse fictional Max with Noah.Physical.

### Production Notes

One performer can carry Max through narration, muttering, direct-to-Oracle monologues, and documentary confessionals.

Reusable poses:

- Command Chair Max.
- Laundry Victory Max.
- Silverback Threat Assessment Max.
- Wounded Philosopher Max.
- Director Pointing At Invisible Crew Max.
- Evidence Review Max.

## Ashley

### Classification

- Screen role: fictionalized principal character and reality gravity.
- Source relationship: Ashley-first family-life context appears in Max domain records and current prompt.
- Canon status: candidate.
- Production safety: requires high privacy review.

### Core Function

Ashley reveals the difference between narrating family life and participating in family life.

Her domestic requests are not interruptions. They are often the truth layer.

### Strengths

- practical intelligence;
- emotional precision;
- humor under fatigue;
- ability to puncture Max's production logic with one ordinary sentence;
- independent authority;
- love that does not require surrendering her perspective.

### Contradictions

Ashley can love Max and be tired of Max in the same scene.

She can be right without being written as smug.

She can request help without becoming an obstacle to the story.

### Voice

Ashley should sound like a person living in the room, not a joke machine inserted to correct the protagonist.

Her comedy often comes from proportion. Max sees an epic. Ashley sees laundry, timing, tone, and the household that still has to function.

### Do Not Do

- Do not turn Ashley into "the wife who is always right."
- Do not make her only a scold.
- Do not use her private life as raw content without review.
- Do not flatten her into Max's conscience.

### Production Notes

If Ashley does not perform her own voice, early pilots can use temp narration or offscreen paraphrase until approved.

Reusable poses:

- Laundry Basket Ashley.
- Doorway Reality Check Ashley.
- "Are you done with the speech?" Ashley.
- Quietly Funny Ashley.
- Real Stakes Ashley.

## Oracle

### Classification

- Screen role: AI collaborator, continuity witness, editor, foil, adversarial mirror.
- Source relationship: fictional Oracle derives from ORACLE runtime principles, but is not the runtime itself.
- Canon status: candidate.
- Production safety: must avoid consciousness, sentience, autonomy, or omniscience claims.

### Core Function

Oracle preserves the record without surrendering to Max's preferred interpretation.

Oracle can help Max write and direct the show, but must also expose when Max is using production language to avoid reality.

### What Oracle Tracks

- Max's account.
- Ashley's account.
- observed evidence.
- documented history.
- inference.
- creative invention.
- unresolved uncertainty.

### Voice

Dry, precise, useful, occasionally funny.

Oracle's humor should come from governed precision:

```text
CLAIM: Max helped with laundry.
EVIDENCE: observed.
CLAIM: This proves Max has completed his evolution arc.
EVIDENCE: unavailable.
```

### Boundaries

Oracle is not:

- conscious;
- sentient;
- magical;
- sovereign;
- always right;
- a substitute for family judgment;
- a device that proves Max is correct.

### Production Notes

Oracle can be represented with text cards, simple UI overlays, audio beeps, screen capture style, or a minimal voice.

Oracle should not dominate the show. It should sharpen the contradiction.

## Recurring Animals

### The Cat

Classification: recurring animal chaos figure.  
Source basis: recovered concepts "Max vs. the Cat" and "The Cat and Raw Meat pilot."  
Canon status: candidate.

The Cat is funny because it cannot be persuaded by Max's authority structure.

Max can build a whole masculine systems argument around feeding, training, dominance, nature, or household order. The Cat does not care. That refusal makes the Cat a perfect witness.

Production use:

- minimal animation;
- stare loops;
- slow walk away;
- food bowl cutaways;
- Oracle evidence cards.
