## Author's Note (the only part written from nothing — because it's about the act, not the man)

This book is not invented. Every fact, every story, every line that claims to be
about my father comes from me — from memory, from the people who knew him, from
what actually happened. Where I'm unsure, it will say so. Where something is felt
rather than known, it will say that too. Nothing here pretends to be more certain
than it is.

That rule exists because the whole reason I'm writing this is the same reason I
built ORACLE: to keep what matters from disappearing — and to keep it *true* while
it's kept. A father deserves to be remembered as he was, not as a story smoothed
over to sound good.

So this is a witness, not a performance.

## Prologue — The Recorder

There is a sound I keep reaching for and not finding.

I don't have it on a drive. I can't search it. It isn't in any archive, and that
absence is the whole reason this book exists — and the reason I built a machine
whose only job is to keep things from disappearing. I built the machine first.
The book is what the machine was always for.

I am writing this on a night I felt my father in the room. Not as a ghost, not as
a metaphor I'm dressing up after the fact — as a plain fact of the body, the way
you feel someone before you turn around. I said a true thing out loud before my
brain could make it smaller: *I feel my dad in this moment. This is why witness
matters.* I didn't plan to say it. It left my mouth on its own. That is how I knew
it was real.

A son spends a long time learning that memory is not a vault. It's a tide. It
takes things back a little at a time — the exact pitch of a voice, the order
events happened in, the throwaway phrase you'd give anything to hear once more —
and it never announces what it's taking. So I am doing the one thing I know how to
do against a tide: I am writing it down. Carefully. Honestly. Marking what I'm
sure of and what I only feel, because he deserves to be remembered as he was, not
as the convenient version.

This is not a eulogy and it is not a monument. It's a record kept by someone who
loved him, made while there is still enough of him in me to get it right.

So I'll start where the truth is, and I'll add to it as it comes back. Dad — this
one is yours.

— Noah

## How we write this (the method)

1. You tell me something true — a memory, a fact, a phrase he said, a photo, a day.
2. I shape it into prose in his book, keeping your voice, not replacing it.
3. Anything I'm unsure of gets marked `[unverified]` until you confirm it.
4. I never add events, quotes, or details that didn't happen. If a chapter is
   thin, it stays thin until you fill it. Empty is honest; invented is not.
5. You can drop screenshots, voice notes, scanned photos, letters — provenance
   preserved (this is literally the Witness Artifact lane: `/witness`, `/screenshot-drop`).

## Things to settle first (so the book takes the right shape)

- His name, and what people called him.
- Is this written to keep him close, or to keep him from disappearing? (living / passed)
- What kind of book: his **life** (biography), **us** (you and him), a **letter** to him,
  or a **collection** of stories + photos + his own words.
- Who else can feed it: mom, siblings, his friends, his own recordings.

## Structure (chapter shells — they fill with your truth, not my guesses)

### Part I — Who he was
> Prompts to pull the real thing out (answer any, in any order, out loud is fine):
- His hands. What did they look like, what did they do, what did they fix or hold.
- A phrase he said so often it became his. Say it the way he said it.
- His smell. The room, the car, the jacket — what brings him back instantly.
- What he did for work, and how he felt about it.
- The thing he was proud of. The thing he never talked about.

### Part II — Stories (the ones that actually happened)
- The first memory of him you can reach.
- A time he showed up for you when it counted.
- A time he was wrong, or hard, or human. (The book is truer with this in it.)
- The funniest thing he ever did.
- An ordinary day with him you'd live again — the boring, perfect kind.

### Part III — What he gave me
- Something you do exactly like him, without meaning to.
- A belief or rule of his you still carry.
- Something you forgave him for. Something you hope he'd forgive you for.

### Part IV — The recorder, the absence *(your root signal)*
- The recorder. The missing archive. Why preserving him became the work.
- What you wish you'd asked him. What you'd ask now.
- Tonight: building this live, feeling him in the room. (Already witnessed.)

### Part V — His own voice
- Quotes, letters, recordings, texts — anything in *his* words, exactly as said.
- A page left for him. What you'd want him to read first.
